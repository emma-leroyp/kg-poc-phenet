Which environment gives the best yield?

Pick a few genotypes and see which trial location gave them the best average yield: a genotype-by-environment comparison, one of the questions a breeding programme actually needs answered.

Example: pick two or three candidate lines and see which site would have suited them best.

Yield is synthetic. The real trial in this dataset measured grain quality (protein, hardness, dough tests), never yield. See data assessment for why. The locations and genotypes are real; only the yield numbers are made up to demonstrate the query.

Do the passport descriptors match the field data?

Every genebank accession has a passport descriptor, an official value recorded when the variety was registered (say, "this line is awned"). This check compares that value against what field trials actually measured, and flags disagreements.

Example: genotype APACHE's passport says its awn type is "awnless". Field trials recorded its awn type 24 times. If 23 say "awnless" and 1 says "awned", that's a 96% match, shown as "some inconsistency" rather than a clean match, because that one reading could be a scoring mistake, or something real worth checking.

What "Verdict" means:
  • Numeric traits (kernel weight, plant height): match means the field average falls within the accepted range of the passport value; mismatch means it doesn't. The exact range used is shown in the results.
  • Categorical traits (awn type, grain colour): each genotype was measured many times. fully consistent means every measurement agreed with the passport value; some inconsistency means at least one didn't. The exact match rate is always shown next to it.

What do we know about a genotype, and where was it used?

Pick a genotype to see its accession number, taxonomy, every trait measured for it, and every trial location it was used at, real and synthetic tagged separately. It also shows a real gap: release date and place of origin, which this dataset can't answer.

Example: pick RE05007 to see its accession number, its taxonomic ID (a real link to the NCBI Taxonomy database), and where it was used.

Release date is never available here. That's not a bug: FAIDARE simply doesn't record it. Answering that for real would need an external source, like a national variety register or genebank passport database. See schema design for detail.
